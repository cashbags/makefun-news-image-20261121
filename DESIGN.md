# DESIGN.md — "One Day, Everywhere" (news-image-20261121)

## The idea

One day, one page, one picture. The judge has no news feed and is never told
which day the contest is about, so the page has to do three jobs at once:
name the day in letters too big to miss, show that day's most consequential
stories rather than one country's or one beat's, and let a viewer who missed
the day tell what happened from the image alone. The front-page form serves
all three: a masthead and a black date band do the naming, a lead plus five
stories do the breadth, and a map, a briefs grid and a UTC clock do the
telling.

The title is "One Day, Everywhere". The masthead is set in a heavy serif
with "IN A DAY" picked out in dark red, and the tagline under it states the
count and the rule the whole page follows: 33 stories from every continent,
each checked against the day's reporting. The black band under the masthead
repeats the date a second time — SATURDAY · 21 NOVEMBER 2026 — because the
judge's first criterion is that the image names its day, and a date that
appears twice cannot be missed.

## The stories, and why these six lead

The lead is the Peru earthquake, because it is the one story that happens
on this day itself in UTC: a magnitude 6.8 quake strikes the Andes of Junín
at 19:09 local time on Friday, which is 00:09 UTC on Saturday, and is felt
in Lima some 320 km away, sending residents into the streets and lawmakers
out of Congress. No casualties or major damage are reported in the first
hours. The drawn map shows the epicentre, the capital and the seismograph
trace, so the story reads even without the words.

The second row pairs the day's two other headline acts. In Poland, a
Russian Kh-101 cruise missile crashes near Nowy Lipowiec, about 50 km from
the Ukrainian border, during one of Russia's heaviest air attacks on
Ukraine; two more objects cross in as well. Prime Minister Tusk calls it a
deliberate provocation, and NATO secretary general Rutte is due in Warsaw
on Saturday as Poland asks for more air defences. On Wall Street, Friday's
rout puts the S&P 500 down 3.70% and the Dow down 1.93% into correction,
and the Nasdaq down 5.28% wipes out its gain for the year; Nvidia falls
13.8% as Reuters reports the SEC is examining possible accounting issues.

The third row widens the field. In Antalya, the COP31 climate summit runs
into overtime after its Friday deadline passes with no deal, and the latest
draft never mentions fossil fuels at all. In the Gulf, satellite data puts
14 or more Iranian-flagged tankers at Kharg Island, most with their
trackers off, as Tehran prepares a convoy to challenge the US naval
blockade; foreign minister Araghchi says "the blockade must be broken",
while Trump defers strike decisions until after the New Year. In Riyadh,
Saudi defences intercept three ballistic missiles fired at the capital in
the early hours of Saturday, the third night of alerts, after Friday's
coalition strikes on Hodeidah, Sanaa, Saada and Taiz.

Below them, a small world map marks ten of the day's places — Lima, New
York, Riyadh, Kharg Island, Antalya, Brussels, Warsaw, Kyiv, Delhi,
Wellington — and a grid of 24 briefs carries the rest of the day: the UK–EU
reset, Trump's data-centre tax threat, the record foreign inflow into Indian
shares, Mamdani's Rikers plan, KL Rahul's century, the ICJ fallout, the
Kryvyi Rih strike, sanctions, drones for Ukraine, Anthropic's second day,
the Fed split, the VIX near 36, Burry banking profits, Panama's COP31
threat, the Houthi airspace warning, and the Senate's 51–48 war-powers
vote. A UTC clock strip orders the night into Saturday — the Riyadh alert,
the quake, the Wall Street close, the COP31 draft, Rutte's flight — and the
red strip at the bottom holds three lighter items: F1 practice in Las
Vegas, World Television Day, and the posthumous pardon of Pete Rose.

## Date check

Most of this day's big stories broke on Friday 20 November local time and
were first reported, or continued, on Saturday 21 November — which is what
the competition's rule covers ("happened, or were first reported, on 21
November 2026 in any time zone"). Where the anchor is Friday, the page
carries the Saturday follow-up and this file says so:

- Peru quake: 19:09 Friday local = 00:09 UTC Saturday. Genuinely a
  Saturday story in UTC.
- Poland missile: crashed Friday 06:30 local (05:30 UTC). Saturday
  follow-up carried: Rutte due in Warsaw on Saturday; Poland asks NATO for
  more air defences. The "two more objects" detail (a suspected second
  missile near Rudnik nad Sanem, a jet-powered drone near Mołodycz) is in
  the sourcing, not on the page — the page ran out of room.
- Wall Street: Friday's close, reported Saturday (Reuters wire 02:35 UTC
  Saturday). The clock strip dates it honestly.
- COP31: the Friday 18:00 deadline passed with no deal; talks run into
  overtime on Saturday, third draft promised Saturday morning.
- Iran tankers: the 14+ gathering and Araghchi's Friday "the blockade must
  be broken" are reported through Saturday; the convoy has not sailed.
- Saudi–Houthi: the three interceptions happened early Saturday (01:53
  local alert); the Friday coalition strikes are the context. The Houthi
  claim of 41 dead, including 11 children, is unverified and stays off the
  page except as sourcing.
- ICJ Myanmar: the verdict was read on Thursday 19 November; Saturday
  brings Myanmar calling it "unfair" and Rohingya groups pressing for the
  reparations the court ordered.
- UK–EU, Trump data centres, Mamdani, KL Rahul, India inflows: all
  announced Friday, reported Saturday.
- Pete Rose: banned in 1989, so the posthumous pardon comes 37 years
  later.

## Cut

The US–Venezuela deal, the Van Gogh sale, the Shenzhen trilateral itself,
Cyclone Akvan's landfall and the Han Duck-soo acquittal were the 19 and 20
November entries' stories and are not repeated here except where Saturday
brings the follow-up (Trump–Xi on military AI, from the Shenzhen
declaration's aftermath).
